# Google Business Profile competitor benchmarking: what owners should compare

**Research date:** 2026-11-24

**Scope:** Practical owner questions, metrics, visuals, caveats, and data-access boundaries for comparing a local business with competitors on Google Business Profile (GBP).

## Executive Summary

Build the benchmark in three layers: **(1) a fair local competitor cohort, (2) publicly observable reputation/profile signals, and (3) owner-only GBP performance data.** Do not use one composite score or a national “average”: Google says local results depend on relevance, distance, and prominence, while current research shows review and completeness benchmarks vary materially by category and market density. [Source #4] [Source #17] [Source #20]

The highest-value owner comparison is not rating alone. Pair rating with review volume, recent velocity, 1–5-star distribution, response coverage/speed, and recurring review themes. Google documents rating and review count as public summary measures; review-level data is needed for distribution and temporal analysis. [Source #2] [Source #7] Competitor calls, directions, views, and website clicks are not available through the public GBP surface, so use geo-grid visibility as a public proxy for discoverability—not as an “impressions” or conversion equivalent. [Source #1] [Source #13]

## Data-access legend

- **Public:** Visible on the GBP/Search/Maps surface, e.g., average rating, total review count, review text/dates, owner replies, categories, hours, and photos. [Source #2] [Source #3] [Source #4]
- **Owner-only:** Requires a verified profile and the associated Google account: GBP Views, Searches, Calls, Directions, Website clicks, Bookings, and native Profile Strength. [Source #1] [Source #5]
- **Derived:** Calculated or observed through third-party tools, e.g., geo-grid ranks, Share of Local Voice (SoLV), review-velocity history, response timing, sentiment themes, and category percentiles. [Source #13] [Source #17] [Source #19] [Source #21]

## Recommended section order for an owner-facing report

1. **Cohort and as-of date** — 3–8 named peers, matching rules, and median/IQR reference band.
2. **Local visibility** — geo-grid map plus SoLV/ARP/ATRP trend.
3. **Owner demand capture** — Views, Calls, Directions, Website clicks, Bookings, and action rates; clearly marked owner-only.
4. **Reputation scorecard** — rating, count, velocity, distribution, and latest-review date.
5. **Response and themes** — response coverage/speed by star band, then top problems and praise with quotes.
6. **Profile and photos** — common completeness checklist and photo-category coverage.
7. **Trend appendix** — complete-month/YoY comparisons, sample sizes, and annotations.

This order answers “who are we really competing with?” before “how are we doing?”, and prevents owner-only action data from being presented as a competitor benchmark.

## Standard definitions to keep comparisons honest

- **Snapshot date:** Capture every public profile measure on the same date.
- **Review velocity:** New reviews in the trailing 28 or 90 days, optionally normalized to reviews per 30 days. Keep text and rating-only reviews separately identifiable.
- **Response rate:** Reviews with an owner reply divided by reviews received in the chosen period; show overall and by star band.
- **Theme share:** Written reviews mentioning a theme divided by written reviews reviewed; allow multi-label themes and show the base.
- **Action rate:** A specific GBP action divided by GBP Views, multiplied by 1,000. Treat it as directional because Views is not a perfectly deduplicated person count. [Source #1]
- **Cohort benchmark:** Prefer median and interquartile range over a mean dominated by a chain or outlier.
- **Time windows:** Use 28 days for momentum in higher-volume businesses, 90 days for lower-volume or theme analysis, complete months for GBP actions, and same-period YoY for seasonality. These are recommended operating windows, not Google rules.

## Conflicting or non-comparable information

- **“20 reviews” is a consumer-trust signal, not a universal ranking threshold.** BrightLocal’s 2026 US survey found 47% of consumers would not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, but Local Falcon’s Q4 2025 3-Pack analysis found category P10 review counts ranging from 1 to 227 and very different medians. These answer different questions. [Source #9] [Source #17]
- **Response-rate benchmarks are not interchangeable.** BrightLocal’s 51% figure is a survey of 1,002 US small-business owners; SOCi’s 46.9% is a Google-response benchmark from its multi-location dataset of 2,751 brands and roughly 350,000 locations. Different populations and denominators mean they should not be averaged. [Source #10] [Source #20]
- **Photo impact is not a guaranteed ranking lever.** Google recommends category-specific photos, and Whitespark’s expert survey includes photo signals, but Sterling Sky’s field analysis reports mixed effects and stronger relevance in visual industries. [Source #6] [Source #8] [Source #12]
- **Expert surveys and observational studies are not algorithm documentation.** Whitespark explicitly says its 47 experts have no special access to Google’s algorithm; Sterling Sky and Local Falcon report correlations. Google itself only confirms broad local-ranking factors. [Source #4] [Source #8] [Source #12] [Source #17]

## Confidence Notes

- **High confidence:** Current GBP metric definitions, owner-only access, rating calculation/lag, review replies being public, profile-strength access, and Google’s broad local-ranking factors come from Google’s own documentation. [Source #1] [Source #2] [Source #3] [Source #4] [Source #5]
- **Moderate confidence:** Review velocity/recency, profile completeness, response coverage, and category-specific review benchmarks are supported by multiple current vendor sources, but vendor samples and scoring methods differ. [Source #8] [Source #10] [Source #12] [Source #17] [Source #18] [Source #20]
- **Lower confidence / directional:** NLP theme comparisons, proprietary completeness scores, and any causal claim that a single change improved rank. Validate themes with human review and use rank studies as directional evidence only. [Source #17] [Source #19] [Source #21]
- **Recency limitation:** ReviewTrackers’ detailed public benchmark report found in search was based on Jan–Oct 2022 data and published Dec 2022. Its current product pages are useful for metric definitions, but the old percentages are not used as 2026 targets. [Source #18] [Source #19]
- **Access note:** Sterling Sky pages were read through a Google Translate proxy because the origin presented a bot challenge; canonical URLs are cited below.

## Open Questions

1. **PricingTrade:** No identifiable, accessible authoritative local-search source was found at pricingtrade.com; searches returned no relevant material. If a specific PricingTrade report was intended, the URL or title is needed.
2. **Exact competitor action benchmarks:** There is no public source for competitor GBP Calls, Directions, Views, or Website clicks. Any vendor claiming this needs a documented methodology and data-access explanation.
3. **Competitor response timing and complete review histories:** Usually require a monitoring vendor or repeated public snapshots; platform surfaces may not expose a complete export.
4. **Owner-specific benchmark selection:** Category, market density, storefront/service-area model, and target keywords are needed before choosing the right percentile cohort.
5. **Harmonized response-rate denominator:** Providers may use all reviews, period reviews, Google-only, or multi-platform reviews. Fix the definition before comparing numbers.
